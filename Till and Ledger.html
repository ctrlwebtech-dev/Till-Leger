Introduction

1111

This document provides the design and structure rationale behind our digital information system’s (IS) resources for Scenario 2: SME Digital Transformation – Retail and Small Business. Small and medium businesses operating in the South African retail industry usually operate using paper documentation, point-of-sale systems that do not necessarily work together, and disparate spreadsheets. Although such processes are sufficient for trading, they introduce operational inefficiencies, errors, security risks as required by POPIA, and pose serious constraints for the growth of the business.

Our team has been tasked with developing digital websites as IS consultants to ensure that the digital transformation of small and medium enterprises occurs without too much friction. The main aim of this information system is to provide a practical understanding of complex IS theories and how to implement them within the day-to-day running of businesses. It targets non-technical retail owners and administrators of the business.

The intended audience for the tool includes owners of small retail businesses, managers of stores, and administrative workers that have low levels of knowledge of IT issues. To maximize the user experience of navigation, the website features a hierarchy of content, diagrams and jargon-free language. In each section the principles of IS studies, such as the Data, Information, Knowledge, Wisdom (DIKW) pyramid, the analytics maturity frameworks, and the Atomicity, Consistency, Isolation, Durability (ACID) properties are translated into practical situations of retail business, thus showing their value.

Website Structure and Navigation

Entry Point of the Website (Landing Page):

  • •Introduction: It acts as the immediate entry point. Here we establish the SME retail scenario; highlight some common problems faced by SMEs (like manual book-keeping, not integrating POS system and disruptions due to power load shedding); and establish the core objective of our Information Systems consulting artifact.
  • •Conclusion: It is placed on the landing page right away to give the store owners some executive summary of key findings from the project.

Main Navigation Bar (Core Topics):

  • Data (Topic 1: Data & Information Management): Discusses the DIKW pyramid, the 7 information quality dimensions, and presents our Crow's Foot ERD database design and an interactive sales dashboard prototype.→
  • POPIA (Topic 2: Data Ethics, Privacy & Compliance): Discusses customer data protection in accordance with South African POPIA law, regulated by the CIA Triad and the AAA security model.→
  • Decisions (Topic 3: AI & Decision Support Systems): Leads users through the 5-stage Analytics Maturity Ladder, internal and external DSS data sources, and Herbert Simon's 4-stage decision-making process for inventory management.→
  • Systems (Topic 4: Enterprise Systems & Integration): Analyzes the Point-of-Sale (POS) and ERP system capabilities, and compares the Cloud (SaaS) and On-Premises approaches in order to avoid siloed operations.→
  • Customise (Topic 5: System Customisation & SDLC): Offers a 9-stage customising scale, API integration procedures, and a 6-stage SDLC checklist stressing offline trade continuity during power outages.→
  • Sources (Academic References): Features a fully formatted Harvard reference list, validating all theoretical models according to the relevant academic literature.→

Conclusion

The objective behind this project was to create an accessible and research-based digital tool to tackle Scenario 2: SME Digital Transformation. Small and medium sized retailers often encounter difficulties regarding keeping written records, securing data, and managing their inventories. By following the structured process, the project was able to meet important milestones in all the areas:

  • •Data & Information Management (Topic 1): A DIKW hierarchy was created along with an ERD that ensured there was no data redundancy and improved inventory visibility.
  • •Data Ethics, Privacy and Compliance with POPIA (Topic 2): Protected customer loyalty data with the help of the CIA Triad, AAA security framework and statutory compliance mechanisms.
  • •Artificial Intelligence and Decision Support Systems (Topic 3): Demystified business analytics with a 5-level maturity ladder and explained how Herbert Simon’s 4-step decision-making model helps avoid expensive stockouts at the retail level.
  • •Enterprise Systems and Integration Issues (Topic 4): Weighed the benefits and disadvantages of Point-of-Sale and cloud (SaaS) integration to eliminate operational silos.
  • •Modification of Information Systems (Topic 5): Created an API-based system customization checklist based on the SDLC.

In conclusion, digital transformation is not limited only to big firms.

Through the provision of user-friendly tools, a strong data infrastructure, and decision-making systems, even small retail firms can be able to enjoy operational resilience, compliance, and scalability within the South African retail environment.
